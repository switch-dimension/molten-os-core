# Plain-language glossary

Use these definitions whenever you **speak or write to the user** (chat, `AskQuestion` prompts, `product-score-*.md`). Do not assume they know startup finance jargon or have read any particular book.

In **internal rubric tables** you may use the short forms in parentheses after the plain term once per section, e.g. "lifetime value (LTV)".

## User output rules (non-negotiable)

**Never** put these in chat, `AskQuestion` labels, or `product-score-*.md`:

**Instead:** describe the *check* in plain language (what you looked for and what the evidence showed). Framework names live only in `[research.md](research.md)` and the **Internal reference** column in `[scoring.md](scoring.md)` — agent-only.

## Customer discovery (how to interview — standalone)

Use this when running Round 2–3 or recommending experiments. Do **not** name a methodology; teach the moves.

**Goal:** Learn whether real people in the target segment already feel the problem and pay (time or money) to cope — without pitching your solution.

**Ask about:**

- Their **current workflow** and tools today (spreadsheets, competitor, doing nothing)
- **Past behaviour:** "Walk me through the last time this happened — what did you do?"
- **Cost of the problem:** money spent, hours lost, workarounds built, things they've tried and dropped

**Do not treat as evidence:**

- "I love it" / "I would use that" / "Great idea" (especially from friends, family, advisors)
- Hypotheticals with no recent example
- Your pitch getting nods — that's politeness, not demand

**Treat as stronger signal:**

- They **already spend** (money, reputation, or serious time) on a workaround
- **Strangers** in the segment describe the problem **before you mention your product**
- Costly commitment: deposit, pre-order, paid pilot, letter of intent, repeat deep conversations

**If they haven't interviewed yet**, recommend a concrete next step, e.g. *"Book 10 conversations with [specific role] — ask only about their last week handling [problem]; don't demo until call 8+"* — not "run Mom Test interviews."

## Unit economics (criterion 6)


| Term                                   | Plain language                                                 | One-line definition                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CAC**                                | **Cost to acquire a customer**                                 | Total sales + marketing spend to win one paying customer (ads, commissions, outbound time, etc.).                                                                                                                       |
| **LTV**                                | **Lifetime value** (or **what a customer is worth over time**) | Revenue (or gross profit) you expect from one customer over the relationship — not just the first purchase.                                                                                                             |
| **LTV:CAC** (or **3:1 rule of thumb**) | **Lifetime value vs acquisition cost**                         | For each £/$ spent acquiring customers, you want roughly **3×** back in lifetime value — a sanity check that paid growth can work. Example: if it costs £100 to acquire someone, they should be worth ~£300+ over time. |


**How to ask in interviews (Round 5):**

> "Rough bottom-up math: how many customers × realistic price × what % you can actually win. For each customer, is what they pay you over time **at least about 3×** what it costs you to get them (ads, sales time, etc.)?"

## Other terms that often need a gloss


| Term          | Plain language                                                                                                       |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **GTM**       | Go-to-market — how you find, sell to, and deliver to customers                                                       |
| **TAM**       | Total addressable market — "how big is the whole pie" (prefer **bottom-up**: your segment × price × realistic share) |
| **Beachhead** | First small segment you can dominate before expanding                                                                |
| **LOI**       | Letter of intent — a non-binding signal a buyer is serious                                                           |
| **MVP**       | Smallest test of the riskiest assumption (landing page, concierge, pre-order — not necessarily a full product)       |
